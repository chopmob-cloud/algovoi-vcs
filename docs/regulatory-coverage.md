# Regulatory coverage

The evidence chains support the documentary requirements in the frameworks below. Your counsel certifies
compliance; the suite provides the tamper-evident, independently verifiable evidence that certification
rests on.

| Framework | Provision | Evidence the suite provides |
|---|---|---|
| HIPAA | 164.312(b) audit controls | Signed tamper-evident read-access log: principal, record, granted or denied, reason; exportable for SIEM and GRC |
| HIPAA | 164.312(c)(1) integrity | Write-once content-addressed archive; the Falcon-1024-signed chain breaks on any alteration |
| GDPR | Art 17 right to erasure | Signed erasure tombstones: content crypto-shredded, a signed proof the record existed survives |
| GDPR | Art 5(1)(f) integrity and confidentiality | ML-KEM-1024-sealed records at rest; offline-verifiable chain integrity |
| eIDAS | Qualified timestamps | RFC-3161 tokens from an independent TSA; air-gap record falls back to a signed chain with no external call |
| 21 CFR Part 11 | 11.10(a) validation documentation | IQ/OQ/PQ self-validation pack generated by the Compliance Command Center on installation |
| Long-term retention | Decades-scale verifiability | Falcon-1024 and ML-DSA-65 keep evidence verifiable after the post-quantum migration |

## What this does not claim

- **It is not a certification.** The suite produces evidence; an assessor or your counsel decides what
  that evidence satisfies.
- **It is not a legal opinion on retention periods.** Retention schedules are yours to configure; the
  suite enforces them and proves it did.
- **Timestamping depends on the TSA.** An RFC-3161 token attests that a TSA saw the digest at a time.
  If you cannot reach a TSA, the chain records a signed fallback and says so, and the resulting
  evidential weight is lower.
